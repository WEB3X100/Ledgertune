Where this usually starts.

  • Years of spreadsheets and no general ledger.
  • A desktop file nobody wants to keep paying for.
  • Open invoices that do not match the bank.
  • Fear that the history will import as nonsense.

Work included.

  • A written map from the old accounts to the new chart
  • Opening balances on an agreed conversion date
  • Open customers, vendors, invoices, and bills
  • Historical summaries when a full journal import is not worth the noise
  • Bank feed start dates that do not duplicate the opening balance

How the QuickBooks work actually runs.

We pick a conversion date. Activity before that date becomes opening balances and, where needed, summary history. Activity after that date is entered or connected, not imported twice.

What you receive.

  • QuickBooks Online file at the conversion date
  • A cross-reference of old accounts to new accounts
  • A list of records that were not moved, and why

Who it is for.

Businesses leaving Excel, Wave, Xero, or QuickBooks Desktop. A migration is the wrong product if the old file is unreconciled and you expected the errors to disappear on import. That is a cleanup, before or during the move.

Three steps.

Step 1Choose the conversion date and the records worth moving.
Step 2Map accounts and load openings and open items.
Step 3Tie the new file back to the old trial balance or spreadsheet totals.

Questions on this service.

Do you import every old transaction?

Only when that history is in scope and the source is clean enough to trust. Many files are better served by solid opening balances.

Can you move from Excel?

Yes, when the spreadsheet has consistent columns for accounts, dates, and open invoices or bills.
